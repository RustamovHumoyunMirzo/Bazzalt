Audio

An Audio Clip is a sound file imported into the project. An Audio Source is a component that plays a clip. An Audio Listener is the position and orientation from which the player hears the scene. A mixer bus is a named route that lets you adjust several sounds together, such as all music or all sound effects.

Play your first sound

  1. Import a WAV, MP3, FLAC, or Ogg/Vorbis file into Assets. These are shown as Audio Clips. Ogg/Opus, AAC, M4A and WMA are not supported by this decoder setup; convert these to a supported format first.
  2. Select the clip in the Asset Browser. Its inspector shows duration, sample rate and channel count, with Play, Pause, Stop and a seek slider. Double-clicking the clip, or choosing Preview in its context menu, starts a preview without entering Play mode.
  3. In the hierarchy, use Add New > Audio > Audio Listener. A listener is often placed on the gameplay camera or made its child. The editor navigation camera is not an audio listener.
  4. Create an Audio Source from the same menu, or add the component to an existing entity. Drag the clip from Assets into its Audio Clip field, or use the typed asset picker.
  5. For music or a UI sound, set Spatial Blend to 0. For sound coming from a place in the world, use 1. Values between them blend the two paths.
  6. Enter Play. Play On Awake starts the sound once. Enable Loop for repeating ambience or music.

Position and distance

Sources and listeners use their entity's world transform, including parent transforms. Forward is local −Z; up is +Y. Use metres for consistent distance and Doppler behavior. Source scale does not multiply its audio distances.

Min Distance is the full-volume boundary. Max Distance is the silent boundary for the 3D part of the sound. Between them, Linear fades evenly, Logarithmic decreases more quickly near the source, and Custom uses editable distance/gain points. Custom distance 0 means Min Distance and 1 means Max Distance. Gains are between 0 and 1; points must be in increasing distance order. The first and last distances stay at 0 and 1.

Selected spatial sources display their distance spheres and outer cone in the Scene viewport. Source and listener icons are editor-only and never appear in Game output. Inner/Outer Cone angles are full angles in degrees; 360 is omnidirectional. Outer Cone Gain controls how much sound remains outside the cone. Doppler Level changes pitch as a source moves relative to the listener; set it to 0 for music or sounds that must keep a fixed pitch.

Use an Audio Mixer

Double-click a .bmixer file, choose Edit in Audio Mixer from its context menu, or open Window > Audio Mixer. The Audio Mixer panel provides channel faders and a visual routing tree, with automatic asset saving.

  1. In the Asset Browser choose Create > Audio Mixer.
  2. Select the .bmixer asset to edit buses. It starts with Master, SFX and Music. Add buses, give them names, and choose their output/parent bus. Cycles are rejected. Master cannot be deleted.
  3. Assign this asset to the active listener's Audio Mixer field. Audio Source's Output Bus picker then lists the mixer's buses.
  4. Route sources to Music, SFX, or a custom bus. Adjust bus volume, pitch, mute, solo, or pause. Bus changes in this asset are saved automatically; runtime/script adjustments are temporary.

Bus gains multiply through the parent chain. Solo keeps the chosen branches audible; muting a parent still mutes its descendants. Pausing a bus preserves playback cursors. If a source references a missing bus, it safely falls back to Master. Removing a mixer bus reparents its child buses, but references to the removed UUID need reassignment.

Listeners and disabled components

Only one listener is used. The enabled listener with the highest Priority wins; equal priorities are resolved by UUID. With no enabled listener, gameplay audio is silent. Preview playback is 2D and does not require a listener. Listener Master Volume and Muted affect gameplay output. Listener Paused preserves source positions in their clips unless Ignore Listener Pause is enabled. That option does not bypass an explicit source pause, a paused bus, or editor pause.

Disabling or removing an Audio Source destroys its voice. Re-enabling it starts a fresh voice if Play On Awake is enabled. Leaving Play or switching scenes stops old scene voices. Clips and bus references remain serialized in the scene.

Memory and voice limits

Enable Stream From Disk for long music and ambience. Short clips use shared decoded buffers and asynchronous loading. Clips that would require over 64 MiB of decoded PCM are streamed automatically. Changing the Streaming field takes effect the next time a voice starts. All clips are limited to 2 GiB, 8 channels and 24 hours. Mono is recommended for positional sources; stereo is useful for music.

The default limit is 64 logical voices, configurable from scripts. A lower source Priority number is more important: 0 is highest and 256 lowest. When the limit is reached, a less important voice can be stolen. Keep music at a high priority. A source whose voice was stolen does not automatically restart each frame; play it again explicitly if needed.

Scripting

See C++ audio or Lua audio for playback, seeking, fades and runtime mixer control. These APIs do not expose miniaudio, devices, or the engine loop.
