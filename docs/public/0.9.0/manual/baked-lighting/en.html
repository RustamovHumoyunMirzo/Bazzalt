Baked diffuse lighting

Baking calculates lighting before gameplay and saves the result. Bazzalt stores diffuse irradiance in light probes: scene entities that describe the light arriving from different directions at a point. A surface blends the four strongest overlapping probes at its world position. Probes need no UV atlas and can light moving objects.

Prepare a scene

  1. Select static Mesh or Primitive Object entities and enable Contribute GI in their components. Leave moving actors and deforming objects out of the bake snapshot. Geometry participation is per component, without parent inheritance.
  2. Set each contributing light to Baked or Mixed. Realtime lights remain fully realtime and do not contribute to this bake.
  3. Open Window > Lighting. Configure the grid half extent, probes per axis and influence radius, then choose Create probe grid. Move the probes into empty space around the lit surfaces. The initial grid is centered at the scene origin. One probe per axis creates a single probe at the center.
  4. You can also add a Light Probe component to an existing entity. The diffuse-probe resource budget is 65,536 enabled probes. Their Radius is in world meters, independent of entity scale; Intensity adjusts the contribution without rebaking.
  5. Choose Bake lighting. On success, the editor applies the result as one undoable operation and marks the active scene dirty. Save the scene to keep it. Scene cooking and world-cell loading include probe data automatically.

Light modes

ModeRealtime renderingBake contribution
RealtimeDirect light and shadowsNone
BakedNo direct lightDirect and bounced diffuse lighting
MixedDirect light and shadowsBounced diffuse only

Baked lights have no visible effect until probe data is baked and a supported material receives it. Mixed mode keeps direct light out of the probes to avoid counting it twice. Disabled lights and disabled probes do not participate. Clearing baked data does not change light modes.

Quality settings

Samples per probe controls deterministic full-sphere sampling. Increase it to reduce indirect noise. Diffuse bounces ranges from zero to four: zero retains baked direct lighting and occluded ambient input; one also samples diffuse reflection from the first surface a ray hits. Maximum ray distance bounds visibility and bounce queries. Surface bias offsets rays to avoid self-intersections; too large a bias loses thin occluders. Avoid placing probes inside solid objects or directly on surfaces.

Ambient radiance is an explicit constant, linear RGB sky input; the editor sets all three channels to the entered value. It does not sample the scene HDR skybox. Leave it zero when using realtime environment lighting to avoid adding ambient illumination twice. The baker uses opaque, two-sided triangles, constant diffuse colors from primitive colors or material overrides, and a default albedo of 0.5 for imported material data it cannot read. Texture-based albedo, alpha-cutout transmission, emissive mesh lighting and glossy transport are not included.

Preview and persistence

Probe crosses appear with scene gizmos: amber before baking, cyan after baking. Selecting a probe shows its influence radius. Lit materials receive per-fragment baked lighting in Scene and Game views; see Material support below.

Probe values remain world-oriented when the entity rotates. Moving a baked probe relocates its stored lighting sample; rebake after moving probes or changing geometry, static lights or material colors. Four-probe blending is a distance-based interpolation, not a visibility-aware tetrahedral probe mesh: place probes carefully at room boundaries to limit light leaking.

Cancellation and safe apply

The baker copies scene geometry on the owning thread, then traces a detached snapshot in the job pool. Snapshot construction and validation can take time for large imported models; tracing itself does not mutate the live scene. Cancel leaves old baked data intact. A changed source scene, changed contributors, changed lights or changed probes rejects the completed result; bake again. Baking, clearing and grid creation are disabled by the native bridge during Play. Switching projects or closing the Lighting window cancels its pending bake. A snapshot is limited to one million triangles by default and prepared model files to 128 MiB.

UV lightmaps

Add a Lightmap component to static Primitive Object or Mesh entities, or select them and choose Add lightmaps to selection in Window > Lighting. Resolution is a power of two from 16 to 4096. Padding is the gutter around each triangle chart. Increase resolution if the charts do not fit. Bake lighting writes a non-overlapping triangle-chart irradiance atlas. Runtime reconstructs chart coordinates from the baked world triangles, preserving the model’s original texture UV sets and material textures. This is non-directional diffuse lighting. A matching lightmap replaces probe diffuse at that surface; it is not added twice.

Moving a lightmapped entity invalidates its atlas until it returns to its baked transform or is rebaked. Animated/deformed geometry is unsuitable. Meshes need not have a pre-authored UV2 channel. Each triangle receives an equal-sized chart, so dense models need higher resolution or separate receivers. Disable the component to fall back to probes.

Reflection captures

Create a Reflection Probe in Window > Lighting, or add the component to an entity. Position it in empty space. Resolution (4–256) controls each cubemap face. Radius limits influence; Intensity scales HDR radiance; Blend Distance feathers the edge into the scene environment. Box Projection uses Box Size as world-space half extents for local parallax correction. Bake lighting captures contributing static triangles, analytic lights and the cached HDR environment, then generates GGX-filtered roughness mip levels. Realtime lights appear in the capture as a frozen snapshot; rebake if they change.

The four strongest overlapping captures blend per fragment. Moving a capture disables its stale result until rebaked or restored. Disabling it removes its contribution. Native material BRDF, roughness, AO, clearcoat and environment fallback remain in use. A scene without an environment uses a black fallback, not an extra light.

Material support

Built-in lit materials, original imported glTF PBR materials and cooked source surface shaders receive baked lighting. Imported textures and UV mappings are retained. Unlit materials and GUI overlays remain unlit. Source material compilation adds private engine parameters without adding them to the public material inspector. Existing precompiled opaque material packages must be rebuilt from source to participate; their bytecode cannot be safely rewritten.

Budgets and data lifetime

There is no 64-probe rendering cap. Up to 65,536 diffuse probes are supported as an explicit resource budget. A spatial lookup stores all overlapping influences; excessive overlap uses exact global lookup rather than silently dropping probes. The GPU atlas is bounded by hardware support and 4096×4096 RGBA32F texels. An oversized atlas reports an error and disables baked sampling. Split very large worlds into streamed cells.

MaxBakeTexels limits lightmap and reflection storage together (default 1,048,576, maximum 8,388,608). Lightmap Samples and Reflection Samples control integration quality independently of diffuse-probe samples. Baked vectors are stored losslessly as little-endian float32 data in YAML scene fields. Scene size is limited to 256 MiB; saves publish atomically, retaining the previous file on failure. Settings and results travel with scenes through cooking and streaming.
